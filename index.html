Stop 01 — Overview

What this session covers

CTE instructors are being asked to prepare students for careers that AI is already reshaping — welding shops with automated quality checks, culinary programs pricing menus with forecasting tools, health science labs using AI-assisted diagnostics. This session is a working session, not a lecture: we'll look at where AI genuinely helps hands-on instruction, where it gets in the way, and how to bring it into a shop, lab, or classroom without losing the hands-on skill-building CTE is built on.

You'll leave with a short list of tools worth trying, a way to think about when AI use supports a skill versus replaces it, and language for talking to students and administrators about responsible use.

You'll walk away with
  • A framework for deciding when AI supports instruction versus undercuts the skill being taught
  • Three ready-to-use AI-assisted lesson ideas across different CTE pathways
  • A starter toolkit of AI tools vetted for classroom and shop use
  • Talking points for discussing AI use with students and administrators

Stop 02 — Your Guide

Who's leading the session

Cliff Sullivan

Computer Science and AI Coordinator — West Virginia Department of Education

Cliff has spent 18 years in education, beginning as a New York City Teaching Fellow in the Bronx, where he earned his MST from Fordham University. He now serves as Computer Science and AI Coordinator for the West Virginia Department of Education and is a Code.org CS Foundations facilitator and former STEAM TAC specialist with WVU. Cliff has led innovative makerspace development, bringing 3-D printing, CNC routing, laser cutting, robotics, game design, pottery, and video production to middle school learners. He has also coached multiple after-school robotics programs and a competitive drone team, championing hands-on, future-ready learning across the state.
